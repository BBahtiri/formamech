# ADR 0009: S9 preregistration over a sealed audit service

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-08-24
- Step: S9

## Context

S9 must prevent evidence-role leakage before S10 creates datasets and before S13
creates training artifacts. The open-source project has no independent audit service
that can keep an evaluation campaign sealed on behalf of the implementer. It can,
however, make the campaign immutable and commit to the withheld holdout before any
production artifact exists.

ADR 0001 adds a second load-bearing constraint: the DOE must cover governing derived
ratios, not merely look uniform in primitive parameters. The S1 failure showed that a
raw-parameter design can appear space filling while leaving the high-concentration
part of the executable family almost empty.

## Decision

S9 uses preregistration with a cryptographic holdout commitment rather than claiming
an unavailable sealed audit.

The campaign ceremony begins with `generate_campaign_secrets()`. The factory makes
three independent calls to Python's `secrets.token_bytes(32)` OS-CSPRNG path and
returns the DOE seed, role-allocation seed and holdout salt once in an immutable
secret bundle. The DOE must be generated after this first step because its seed is
one of those outputs; campaign closure therefore requires the same bundle instead of
generating replacement secrets after the design exists. The generator identity is
`python-secrets-token-bytes-32-v1`.

The DOE is generated from domain-separated BLAKE2b Latin-hypercube streams. The
public checkpoint contains only a domain-separated commitment to its seed; the seed
remains with the private reveal. Candidate parameters are evaluated only through a
typed `ExecutableEnvelope`. That canonical object embeds the exact S6
family-membership predicate, non-empty material bounds, non-empty load bounds, the
compiled-family hash, and the fixed fail-closed refusal-policy identifier. Geometry
coordinates are partitioned back through S6; material and load coordinates are
checked with the same exact-name, unit, dimension, and bound discipline.
Every primitive parameter, including a fixed parameter, and every compiled derived
quantity is a declared, fixed-width normalized axis. Fixed primitive axes declare
one stratum and remain visible in every case coordinate mapping. Selection satisfies
preregistered marginal strata,
boundaries, interactions and difficult-region quotas rarest-first, then fills the
remaining budget by weighted maximin distance. Candidate/selection, independent
validation, and guard-band streams have distinct identities; reserves are a
preregistered maximin ranking over the unused candidate stream. Rejected proposals
and negative guard-band cases remain visible with their reasons. The version-one
guard policy targets primitive-bound negatives. Derived-bound and family-membership
rejections remain in the proposal ledger, but are not claimed as targeted guard
strata.

Coverage is a vector, not a score. It records empirical fill distance against an
independently generated admissible validation stream, minimum stratum count,
marginal-stratum coverage, boundary coverage, interaction coverage,
difficult-region coverage, and sorted uncovered or unresolved requirement IDs. No
single geometric percentage is accepted as a substitute. `G9.coverage >= 90%` is the
S9 source-package branch-coverage floor established by the global gate policy.

The five evidence roles are `PILOT`, `TRAIN`, `DEVELOPMENT`, `HOLDOUT` and `LEGACY`.
A case can be assigned exactly once, even if the second write repeats the same role.
Cases joined by geometry lineage, morph seed or a preregistered near-duplicate
tolerance form one transitive `leakage_group_id` and are assigned atomically.
The tolerance is carried by a required, versioned `LeakagePolicy`; a caller cannot
substitute already-materialised group strings for that policy. The built-in DOE
records one pre-role `LeakageDescriptor` per selected or reserve case. Its lineage
identity comes from the exact primitive coordinate identity, its morph-seed identity
comes from the domain-separated proposal stream, and its proximity coordinates are
the normalized primitive-plus-derived DOE axes. The design constructor and campaign
closure independently replay the policy over those descriptors and reject any group
identifier that differs. The policy and descriptors are private because they include
the sealed population, but both are committed by `private_doe_plan_hash` before any
production label is generated.
Production roles are allocated by an exact whole-group partition driven by the second
secret in the same generated campaign-secret bundle. Campaign closure verifies that
the bundle's DOE seed is exactly the seed encoded in the design specification,
replays allocation from its allocation seed, and rejects a supplied ledger that is
merely well-shaped but was not generated by the preregistered allocator. Raw
allocation-seed and holdout-salt parameters are not accepted by the production
closure API. The public ledger retains only a domain-separated seed commitment.
Public reserves cannot share a leakage group with a differently assigned active
case, including a concealed `HOLDOUT` case.

Campaign closure accepts only the exact `ExecutableEnvelope` type. It verifies that
the envelope hash binds the supplied compiled family and that the embedded geometry
membership is the family membership. It then requires the DOE axis vocabulary to
match the complete envelope and replays every selected, reserve, and validation case
as accepted. Guard negatives replay as rejected with identical primitive
coordinates, reason codes, and every derived coordinate that was computable before
rejection. Before returning a checkpoint, closure regenerates the complete coupled
design from that envelope and the secret DOE seed and requires canonical byte
identity, covering selections, reserves, strata, stream identities, proposal
rejections, guard evidence, leakage descriptors, and selection trace. This makes the
envelope an executed closure boundary rather than an opaque hash preimage.
Production-label authorization fails until the exact case and leakage group already
exist in the role registry. Materialized node observations are checked against that
case-level registry, which makes `RANDOM_NODE_SPLIT` observable.

Replacement uses an immutable, role-specific ordered reserve policy. A caller reports
a failure and reason but cannot choose the replacement. Every failure produces an
append-only event naming the next unused reserve or explicit exhaustion. A failed
reserve cannot later re-enter the campaign. Version one permits reserves only for
`PILOT`, `TRAIN` and `DEVELOPMENT`. A failed `HOLDOUT` case remains visible as
exhausted and aborts the campaign; it is never replaced because that would change the
sealed exact-64 coordinate set. The rule is fixed before outcomes exist; later
failures do not change role assignments or hide the failed case.

The production holdout contains exactly 64 unique coordinate mappings with one fixed
schema. Its public record is:

```json
{
  "scheme": "blake2b-256",
  "campaign_hash": "...",
  "n_holdout_points": 64,
  "commitment": "H(salt || canonical_json(sorted_holdout_coordinates))",
  "created_utc": "...Z"
}
```

The holdout salt is the third secret in the same generated campaign-secret bundle.
The DOE seed, allocation seed and salt are pairwise distinct OS-CSPRNG draws.
Structural checks reject short, periodic or obviously low-diversity bytes as
defense-in-depth, but they are not an entropy proof and caller-supplied production
secret bytes are not accepted. The bundle rejects canonical serialization and
pickling so it cannot be swept into a public artifact accidentally. Production
commitment creation accepts the bundle; reveal still accepts the salt persisted from
that bundle.

This is a code-path guarantee against accidental use of caller-chosen secrets, not a
cryptographic attestation of process integrity. Code executing inside the same Python
process can monkeypatch or deliberately bypass private implementation details. That
residual risk is consistent with this ADR's local-holder threat model and is not
represented as an independent sealed audit.

The ceremony operator must persist salt and coordinates in an ignored private reveal
file and is responsible for that file's access controls. They never enter Git,
CP-ENVELOPE, gate artifacts, tests or training inputs. Reveal comparison is
constant-time. The commitment file must be
clean, tracked and added in exactly one Git commit. At artifact creation, the live
filesystem ordering is captured in a canonical record committed atomically with the
artifact batch. Later verification consumes that persisted record and separately
checks clean first-add Git ancestry; it never treats fresh-checkout mtimes as
historical evidence. Legacy exclusion is based on the explicit role, never a path
convention.

`CP-ENVELOPE` schema 1.1 recomputes its campaign identity from one canonical binding
over content hashes for the compiled family, pilot evidence, final executable
envelope, private DOE plan, role registry, replacement policy and public non-holdout
case manifest, together with the DOE-seed commitment, secret-generator identifier,
derived axes and fixed role/reserve budget. The unsalted seed commitment and private
plan hash are dictionary-confirmation oracles for a guessable seed; they are safe only
under the bound 256-bit OS-CSPRNG creation path. They are commitments, not entropy
amplifiers. CP-ENVELOPE discloses no holdout coordinate. S10 consumes the strict
canonical public manifest; it does not regenerate the hidden DOE from a public seed.

## Instantiation status

This ADR fixes the S9 contract shape. The DogBone family, material point, exact
10%–20% strain-controlled load envelope and solver recipe, role budget, DOE quota
policy, leakage tolerance, reserve distribution, public IDs, and private reveal
handling now have owner ratification. The repository still has no production holdout
commitment or `CP-ENVELOPE`; those must be created by the one-shot ceremony rather
than from a fixture. Until the commitment is tracked and the remaining G9 evidence
passes, `G9.commitment-order` is `NOT_RUN`, G9 is not PASS, and S10 must not begin.

## Consequences

- S10 can consume immutable case roles and refuse HOLDOUT cases before constructing
  collocation or normalization data.
- S13 can persist live-mtime evidence when it creates an artifact batch and later
  prove its separate Git-ancestry ordering.
- A fresh checkout cannot reconstruct historical mtimes because Git does not store
  them. The live check is therefore performed at artifact creation and recorded;
  Git first-add ancestry remains independently reproducible from repository history.
- The commitment proves that a later reveal matches the preregistered coordinates. It
  does not prove that the implementer never viewed the private file, and the project
  does not claim an independent sealed audit.
- The recorded generator identity states the required creation procedure and makes a
  change to that procedure alter campaign identity. It cannot prove that hostile
  in-process code left the implementation unmodified.
- Finite empirical fill distance and sampled family membership are evidence over
  their declared streams, not proofs of continuous-domain coverage or injectivity.
- Version-one guard points exercise primitive bounds. The independent validation
  stream and rejection ledger expose derived-bound failures, but do not prove dense
  coverage immediately outside every derived boundary.

## Verification

G9 binds this decision through 1,000-trial role and leakage properties, the named
node-split mutant, holdout-visibility checks, injected replacement failures, exact
commitment/reveal vectors, temporary-repository mtime and Git-order tests,
non-aggregated DOE metrics, strict typing/lint/security checks, and at least 90%
branch coverage over `src/piginot/doe`.
